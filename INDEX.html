Key concepts

The 10 most testable ideas across Chapters 20–22

Ch. 20 — Budgeting

Master budget order

Sales → Production → DM purchases → DL → Overhead → SG&A → Capital expenditures → Cash budget → Budgeted income statement → Budgeted balance sheet. Sales always comes first.

Ch. 20

Production budget formula

Budgeted sales + Desired ending inventory − Beginning inventory = Units to produce. Ending inventory uses next month's sales.

Ch. 20

Cash budget rules

Depreciation is never in the cash budget (it's non-cash). Cash = Beginning + Receipts − Disbursements ± Borrowing/Repayments.

Ch. 20

Bottom-up budgeting

Most useful budgets come from a bottom-up participatory process. Employees provide input. Top-down is less effective and less accurate.

Ch. 21 — Variances

Price vs. quantity variance

Price variance = did we pay more/less per unit of input? Quantity variance = did we use more/less than standard? Excess quantity used ≠ price variance.

Ch. 21

Favorable vs. unfavorable

Unfavorable = actual cost > standard → debit in ledger. Favorable = actual < standard → credit. Price variance → purchasing dept. Quantity/efficiency → production dept.

Ch. 21

Overhead variances

Controllable = Actual OH − Flexible budget OH. Volume = Budgeted fixed OH − Applied fixed OH. Volume variance involves only fixed costs.

Ch. 22 — Resp. accounting

Responsibility center types

Cost center: costs only. Profit center: revenues + costs (selling dept). Investment center: revenues, costs, AND assets. Investment center ≠ profit center.

Ch. 22

ROI components

ROI = Profit margin × Investment turnover. PM = Net income ÷ Sales. IT = Sales ÷ Avg invested assets. Know how to solve for any variable.

Ch. 22

Expense allocation bases

Rent/utilities → square footage. Payroll costs → # employees. Sales-related → sales volume. Indirect expenses benefit multiple departments.

Chapter 20 — Budgeting

Production units = Sales units + Desired ending inv − Beginning inv
DM purchases = DM needed for production + Desired ending DM inv − Beginning DM inv
Ending cash balance = Beginning cash + Total receipts − Total disbursements ± Financing
Cash collections = Cash sales + (credit sales × same-month %) + (prior month credit sales × collection %)
Budgeted sales revenue = Units × Selling price per unit (for each product, then sum)

Chapter 21 — Standard costs & variances

DM Price Variance = (Actual price − Standard price) × Actual qty purchased
Negative result = Favorable  |  Positive = Unfavorable
DM Quantity Variance = (Actual qty used − Standard qty allowed) × Standard price
Standard qty allowed = Standard qty per unit × Actual units produced
DL Rate Variance = (Actual rate − Standard rate) × Actual hours worked
DL Efficiency Variance = (Actual hours − Standard hours allowed) × Standard rate
Standard hours allowed = Standard hrs/unit × Actual units produced
Overhead Controllable Variance = Actual OH − Flexible budget OH
Flexible budget OH = (Std variable OH rate × actual units) + Budgeted fixed OH
Overhead Volume Variance = Budgeted fixed OH − (Standard fixed OH rate × Actual units)
Total Overhead Variance = Actual OH − Standard OH applied
Standard OH applied = (variable + fixed std rate) × standard inputs for actual output

Chapter 22 — Responsibility accounting & ROI

ROI = Net Income ÷ Average Invested Assets
ROI = Profit Margin × Investment Turnover
Profit Margin = Net Income ÷ Sales
Investment Turnover = Sales ÷ Average Invested Assets
Sales = Net Income ÷ Profit Margin (to back-solve)
Dept contribution to overhead = Sales − Direct (controllable) expenses

Common traps

7 specific ways this exam tries to trick you

Depreciation in the cash budget

Depreciation is NEVER in the cash budget. It's a non-cash expense. When you see a list of items including depreciation, always exclude it when computing ending cash balance. Q18 tested this directly.

Quantity variance ≠ price variance

Using more material than standard is a quantity variance. Paying more per unit is a price variance. Q21 stated "actual quantity exceeds standard quantity" and called it a price variance — that's false. Excess quantity = quantity variance, unfavorable.

Investment center ≠ profit center

These are NOT the same thing. Investment centers also control assets and are evaluated on ROI. Profit centers only control revenues and costs. A selling department is a profit center. Q38 tested this: "investment center is another name for profit center" → False.

Volume variance uses only fixed overhead

Volume variance = difference between budgeted fixed OH and applied fixed OH. Nothing to do with variable overhead. Controllable variance covers variable spending + variable efficiency + fixed spending. These two are always on the exam together.

Production budget: use NEXT month's sales for ending inventory

If ending inventory = 25% of next month's sales, you need the following month's number, not the current month. Q10 asked for August production — ending inventory was 25% of September sales (5,560), not August. Easy to grab the wrong column under exam pressure.

Direct expenses are controllable, indirect are not

Q40 said "a department's direct expenses are usually considered uncontrollable costs" → False. Direct (traceable) expenses are controllable by the department manager. Indirect expenses are allocated from shared costs — those are uncontrollable at the department level.

Budgets are short-term (1 year or less)

Q2 said "budgets are long-term financial plans covering more than one year" → False. Budgets typically cover one year or less. Plans that span multiple years are called strategic or long-range plans. Don't confuse with the capital expenditures budget which may plan for asset purchases but is still part of the annual master budget.
